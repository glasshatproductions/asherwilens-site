DESERT DIAMONDS

A natural diamond is a rare sighting.

Treat a natural diamond like the rarest creature in the desert. The campaign starts with paparazzi going to absurd lengths, hiding in cacti, shrubs and pool floats, for a single glimpse of the real thing. Then it hands the binoculars to everyone else: a microsite and social program where real people document their own rare sightings.

01

The insight

The insight

People will go to absurd lengths to glimpse something truly rare. A natural diamond deserves that kind of attention.

The idea

A natural diamond is a rare sighting.

02

The campaign

Print and social executions that cast natural diamonds as a rare species and the people hunting them as obsessive field photographers.

03

Social: built for the feed

The same series recut for Instagram and mobile: 4:5 frames, the line set large enough to read in a scroll, and the photographer right in the middle of the shot.

04

The films

Short films that play the stakeout for laughs: the long lens, the binoculars, the paparazzo who will go anywhere, even underwater, for one glimpse of the real thing.

05

The microsite: Rare Sightings

The campaign lands on a field expedition. The researcher has left; the notes, maps and binoculars are waiting for you.

06

The archive

Every ring becomes a numbered field entry, logged by location and date and classified by species: the round, the pear, the oval, the halo. Each one confirmed.

07

The field reporter program

Owners upload a photo or video of their own ring, name it and say where it was spotted. They get an official field report back, and the best sightings are featured in the archive and on Instagram. The audience becomes the content engine.

08

The full experience

From expedition to field guide to available stones: one page that moves from story to shopping without breaking character.
